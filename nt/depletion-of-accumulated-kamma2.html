Notes on Dhamma

Depletion of Accumulated Kamma

In the sancetana sutta (AN 10.219), the Buddha makes it clear that without experiencing all kamma accumulated in past lives, the attainment of arahantship is not possible:

“I, O bhikkhus, do not say that [there is] the termination of accumulated past [lives] intentional kamma without having experienced [it], and that is in this [present] life, or in the [next] arising or in some other [arisings] in succession. But still I, O bhikkhus, do not say [there is] the making of an end of suffering without having experienced the accumulated past [lives] intentional kamma.”

Now there is a wishful thinking by proponents of defunctable kamma that the results of evil kamma done in past lives do not need to be experienced for one to become an arahant because the results of evil kamma done in past lives can be escaped and be made defunct, like someone who takes birth but somehow manages to escape death. To justify this wishful thinking, they cite a fabricated story in some commentary about the venerable Mahamoggallana being murdered by bandits because of his past evil kamma of killing his parents. The logic is that if the venerable Mahamoggallana had to experience all the evil past lives kamma before becoming an arahant, then he would not have been murdered in this life for having killed his parents in a past life, and so it is not necessary to experience all the evil kamma done in past lives before one can become an arahant, contrary to what the Buddha says in the sancetana sutta. But the Buddha makes it clear that just as death cannot be escaped by one who takes birth, the results of evil kamma cannot be escaped by one who performs evil kamma. As the following dhammapada verses make clear, evil kamma and death are the two liabilities that cannot be escaped:

Neither in the air, nor the middle of the ocean, nor going into an open cleft in a mountain; 127 a place in the world does not exists, where staying one could be released from evil kamma.

Neither in the air, nor the middle of the ocean, nor going into an open cleft in a mountain, 128 a place in the world does not exists, where staying one is not overpowered by death.

So then how is this statement of the Buddha to be understood?

  1. How does the experiencing of the results of accumulated past lives kamma come about in order to make an end of suffering and become an arahant?
  2. What about the results of accumulated present life kamma? Can the arahant escape the results of accumulated present life kamma so that it becomes defunct as the proponents of defunctable kamma claim, or does the arahant have to experience all of the results of accumulated present life kamma as well?

To answer question one: How does the experiencing of the results of accumulated past lives kamma come about in order to make an end of suffering and become an arahant, it is important to look as a similar declaration by the Buddha about the world in the rohitassa sutta (SN 2.26).

yattha kho, āvuso, na jāyati na jīyati na mīyati na cavati na upapajjati, nāhaṁ taṁ gamanena lokassa antaṁ ñāteyyaṁ daṭṭheyyaṁ patteyyanti vadāmī’ti…

Na kho panāhaṁ, āvuso, appatvā lokassa antaṁ dukkhassa antakiriyaṁ vadāmi. Api ca khvāhaṁ, āvuso, imasmiṁyeva byāmamatte kaḷevare sasaññimhi samanake lokañca paññapemi lokasamudayañca lokanirodhañca lokanirodhagāminiñca paṭipadānti.

Where, O friend, there is not being born, not dying, not falling away, not arising, it is not by going, that this end of the world could be known, could be seen, could be attained, I say…

But still, O friend, without having attained the end of the world, there is not the making an end of suffering, I say. For indeed, O friend, it is in this very fathom long corpse, with its perception, with its mind, that I declare the world, the origination of the world, the cessation of the world, and the path leading to the cessation of the world.

Likewise in the kammanirodha sutta (SN 35.146) the Buddha declares kamma as follows:

“navapurāṇāni, bhikkhave, kammāni desessāmi kammanirodhaṃ kammanirodhagāminiñca paṭipadaṃ. taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmīti.”O bhikkhus, I will teach you new and old kamma, the cessation of kamma, and the way leading to the cessation of kamma. Listen to that and do mind it well, I will speak.”

katamañca, bhikkhave, purāṇakammaṃ? cakkhu, bhikkhave, purāṇakammaṃ abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ vedaniyaṃ daṭṭhabbaṃ…pe…. mano purāṇakammo abhisaṅkhato abhisañcetayito vedaniyo daṭṭhabbo. idaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, purāṇakammaṃ.

And what, O bhikkhus, is old kamma? The eye, O bhikkhus, is old kamma, especially conditioned, especially intended, to be seen as something to be felt. …pe…. The mind, O bhikkhus, is old kamma, especially conditioned, especially intended, to be seen as something to be felt. This is called, O bhikkhus, old kamma.

katamañca, bhikkhave, navakammaṃ? yaṃ kho, bhikkhave, etarahi kammaṃ karoti kāyena vācāya manasā, idaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, navakammaṃ. And what, O bhikkhus, is new kamma? O bhikkhus, whatever present action is done by body, speech, or mind. This is called, O bhikkhus, new kamma.

katamo ca, bhikkhave, kammanirodho? yo kho, bhikkhave, kāyakammavacīkammamanokammassa nirodhā vimuttiṃ phusati, ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, kammanirodho.

And what, O bhikkhus, is the cessation of kamma? [That] which touches liberation through the cessation of bodily action, verbal action and mental action. This is called, O bhikkhus, the cessation of kamma.

katamā ca, bhikkhave, kammanirodhagāminī paṭipadā? ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo, seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi, sammāsaṅkappo, sammāvācā, sammākammanto, sammāājīvo, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati, sammāsamādhi ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, kammanirodhagāminī paṭipadā.

“And what, O bhikkhus, is the path leading to the cessation of kamma? It is just this noble eightfold path, that is – right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right samādhi. This is called, O bhikkhus, the path leading to the cessation of kamma.

So then, old kamma pertains to kamma done and accumulated in past lives to be felt via the six senses, new kamma pertains to kamma done and accumulated in this present life, the cessation of kamma refers to that which touches liberation through the cessation of bodily, verbal and mental action. Since kamma is defined as volition which has contact as its origin, the cessation of contact is that which touches liberation through the cessation of bodily, verbal and mental action. This is also said to be the cessation of old kamma, the cessation of new kamma and the cessation of the world. The path leading to the cessation of kamma is just this noble eight-fold path.

An obvious question may be raised that if the depletion of intentional kamma accumulated in past lives is such a gating item for the attainment of arahantship, why doesn’t the Buddha make the wearing away of kamma the purpose of his teaching for bringing about the ending of suffering, similar to the Niganthas? On the contrary, we learn from the mahakotthita sutta (AN 9.13) that manipulation of kamma to wear them away is not the purpose of the life of purity under the Auspicious One:

1 Then the venerable Mahakotthita visited with the venerable Sariputta. Upon arrival, he exchanged friendly greetings with the venerable Sariputta. Having exchanged friendly greetings and cordial talk, he sat down to one side. Having sat down to one side, the venerable Mahakotthita said this to the venerable Sariputta.

2 How about this, O friend, Sariputta, “Let whichever kamma is to be experienced in this life be experienced by me in the after-life.” Is this the purpose for which the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?

3 Certainly not, O friend.

4 Then how about this, O friend, Sariputta, “Let whichever kamma is to be experienced in the after-life be experienced by me in this life?” Is this the purpose for which the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?

5 Certainly not, O friend.

6 How about this, O friend, Sariputta, “Let whichever kamma is to be experienced as pleasant be experienced by me as painful?” Is this the purpose for which the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?

7 Certainly not, O friend.

8 Then how about this, O friend, Sariputta, “Let whichever kamma is to be experienced as painful be experienced by me as pleasant?” Is this the purpose for which the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?

9 Certainly not, O friend.

10 How about this, O friend, Sariputta, “Let whichever kamma is to be experienced when ripened be experienced by me when unripened?” Is this the purpose for which the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?

11 Certainly not, O friend.

12 Then how about this, O friend, Sariputta, “Let whichever kamma is to be experienced when unripened be experienced by me when ripened?” Is this the purpose for which the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?

13 Certainly not, O friend.

14 How about this, O friend, Sariputta, “Let whichever kamma is to be experienced substantially be experienced by me slightly?” Is this the purpose for which the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?

15 Certainly not, O friend.

16 Then how about this, O friend, Sariputta, “Let whichever kamma is to be experienced slightly be experienced by me substantially?” Is this the purpose for which the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?

17 Certainly not, O friend.

18 How about this, O friend, Sariputta, “Let whichever kamma is to be experienced not be experienced by me?” Is this the purpose for which the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?

19 Certainly not, O friend.

20 Then how about this, O friend, Sariputta, “Let whichever kamma is not to be experienced be experienced by me?” Is this the purpose for which the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?

21 Certainly not, O friend.

32 “Now then, for what purpose, O friend, is the life of purity lived under the Auspicious One?”

33 “Indeed, O friend, it is for the knowledge, for the vision, for the attainment, for the realization, for the breakthrough, of that which is unknown, unseen, unattained, unrealized, unbroken through, that the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?”

34 “But what, O friend, is unknown, unseen, unattained, unrealized, unbroken through, for the sake of its knowledge, vision, attainment, realization, and breakthrough that the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One?”

35 “This is suffering”, O friend, is unknown, unseen, unattained, unrealized, unbroken through. It is for the sake of its knowledge, vision, attainment, realization, and breakthrough that the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One.” “This is the origination of suffering”, O friend, is unknown, unseen, unattained, unrealized, unbroken through. It is for the sake of its knowledge, vision, attainment, realization, and breakthrough that the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One.” “This is the cessation of suffering”, O friend, is unknown, unseen, unattained, unrealized, unbroken through. It is for the sake of its knowledge, vision, attainment, realization, and breakthrough that the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One.” “This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering”, O friend, is unknown, unseen, unattained, unrealized, unbroken through. It is for the sake of its knowledge, vision, attainment, realization, and breakthrough that the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One.”

36 Indeed, O friend, it is for the knowledge, for the vision, for the attainment, for the realization, for the breakthrough of this which is unknown, unseen, unattained, unrealized, unbroken through, that the life of purity is lived under the Auspicious One.”

The reason why the Buddha does not single out the depletion of kamma as the solution to suffering like the Niganthas is because kamma is cetana and cetana is only a subset of the sankhara appropriable khandha. So even if one were to wear away all kamma of the past lives in order to be free from their results and not create new kamma, one would still not be freed from suffering since the Buddha declares that all five appropriable khandhas are suffering, not just kamma. In the absence of all new and old kamma, there will remain form, feeling, perception, other types of sankharas, and consciousness which are also suffering. In the nibbedhika sutta (AN 6.63), the Buddha includes kamma as only one of the six dhammas whose cessation is to be known as the penetrative life of purity. Also in the sivaka sutta (SN 36.21), the Buddha gives eight causes of afflictions, with the results of kamma being only one of them. And so even if one could deplete all old kamma, one would still be subject to afflictions from the other seven causes of affliction. Thus the depletion of old kamma is only a necessary part of the solution but not sufficient to bring about the ending of suffering, and so the Buddha rather places emphasis on the depletion of all five appropriable khandhas which he defines as suffering in the four noble truths in order to bring about an ending of suffering. In other words, the depletion of old kamma, being a wholesome dhamma, is already included in the four noble truths so need not to be singled out as stated the by venerable Sariputta in the mahāhatthipadopama sutta:

Just as, O friends, of whatever living beings that walk, the footprint of any of them fits within an elephant's footprint, and the elephants foot is declared the foremost among them with respect to its largeness, in the same way, O friends, whatever wholesome dhammas there are, all of them are included in the four noble truths.

Now the proponents of defunctable kamma, argue that it is impossible to experience all the kamma accumulated in past lives because this samsara has no known beginning and so beings must have accumulated infinite number of kammas in past lives. Their logic is that it must take infinite time to experience the infinite amount of kamma accumulated in the past lives and so attainment of arahantship cannot be possible. And so they purport that all the past lives kamma must become defunct in order to avoid this infinity problem.

But the Buddha proclaims the sankhara appropriable khandha to be any type of sankhara whatsoever, whether past, future or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, far or near, all sankharas and anything pertaining to sankharas like kamma, are included in the sankhara appropriable khandha, whether finite or infinite is inconsequential. And so once all five appropriable khandhas are brought to depletion, all kamma accumulated in past lives also go to depletion.

The mahāsaḷāyatanika sutta, (MN 149) describes how all accumulated five appropriable khandhas go to depletion as follows:

  cakkhuñca kho bhikkhave, jānaṃ passaṃ yathābhūtaṃ, rūpe jānaṃ passaṃ yathābhūtaṃ, cakkhuviññāṇaṃ jānaṃ passaṃ yathābhūtaṃ, cakkhusamphassaṃ jānaṃ passaṃ yathābhūtaṃ, yampidaṃ cakkhusamphassapaccayā uppajjati vedayitaṃ sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā, tampi jānaṃ passaṃ yathābhūtaṃ, cakkhusmiṃ na sārajjati, rūpesu na sārajjati, cakkhuviññāṇe na sārajjati, cakkhusamphasse na sārajjati, yampidaṃ cakkhusamphassapaccayā uppajjati vedayitaṃ sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā, tasmimpi na sārajjati.     tassa asārattassa asaṃyuttassa asammūḷhassa ādīnavānupassino viharato āyatiṃ pañcupādānakkhandhā apacayaṃ gacchanti. taṇhā cassa ponobhavikā nandirāgasahagatā tatratatrābhinandinī, sā cassa pahīyati. tassa kāyikāpi darathā pahīyanti, cetasikāpi darathā pahīyanti, kāyikāpi santāpā pahīyanti, cetasikāpi santāpā pahīyanti, kāyikāpi pariḷāhā pahīyanti, cetasikāpi pariḷāhā pahīyanti, so kāyasukhampi cetosukhampi paṭisaṃvedeti.

But O bhikkhus, one who knows and sees the eye as it has come to be, one who knows and sees forms as they have come to be, one who knows and sees eye-consciousness as it has come to be, one who knows and sees eye-contact as it has come to be, and this feeling which arises in dependence on eye-contact, whether pleasant or painful or neither-painful-nor-pleasant, that too one who knows and sees it as it has come to be, is not tainted in the eye, is not tainted in forms, is not tainted in eye-consciousness, is not tainted in eye-contact, and this feeling which arises in dependence on eye-contact, whether pleasant or painful or neither-painful-nor-pleasant, that too he is not tainted in it.

And for he who abides untainted, unattached, mindful, seeing in accordance with disadvantage, his five appropriable khandhas go to depletion for future [being]; and his thirsting—the renewer of being, accompanied by lusting after delight, delighting now here now there—is abandoned. His bodily stresses are abandoned, his mental stresses are abandoned, his bodily torments are abandoned, his mental torments are abandoned, his bodily fevers are abandoned, his mental fevers are abandoned, and he experiences bodily and mental pleasure.

When the ariya abides knowing and seeing the eye, forms, eye consciousness, eye contact and the feeling born of eye contact as they have come to be, this is abiding in an immeasurable samadhi where measurable kamma accumulated in the past cannot endure, and have to be felt for them to become extinct. As the Buddha makes it clear, one’s thirsting also goes to extinction. With the extinction of thirsting, all previous accumulated kamma also become extinct as described in the khaya sutta (SN 46.26).

“Here, O Udayi, a bhikkhu enculturates the mindfulness awakening factor in dependence on seclusion, in dependence on fading away, in dependence on cessation, maturing in relinquishment, abundant, exalted, immeasurable, free from affliction. For him enculturating the mindfulness awakening factor in dependence on seclusion, in dependence on fading away, in dependence on cessation, maturing in relinquishment, abundant, exalted, immeasurable, free from affliction, there is the abandoning of thirsting. With the abandoning of thirsting, there is the abandoning of kamma. With the abandoning of kamma, there is the abandoning of suffering. Thus, O Udayi, from the extinction of thirsting comes the extinction of kamma. From the extinction of kamma comes the extinction of suffering.”

Same is said about the six remaining awakening factors. As stated in the sancetana sutta, the extinction of suffering is only possible with the depletion of all accumulated kamma from previous lives.

Now to answer question two: What about the results of accumulated present life kamma? Can the arahant escape the results of accumulated present life kamma so that it becomes defunct, as the proponents of defunctable kamma claim, or does the arahant have to experience all of the results of accumulated present life kamma as well?

The answer we get from the nidāna sutta (AN3.34) is that only the kamma done with non-greed, non-hatred and non-delusion as source get abandoned when greed, hatred and delusion are eliminated:

9 There are, O bhikkhus, these three sources for the origination of kamma. What three? Non-greed is a source for the origination of kamma, non-hatred is a source for the origination of kamma, non-delusion is a source for the origination of kamma.

10 Whatever kamma, O bhikkhus, is fashioned by non-greed, born of non-greed, with non-greed as its source, with non-greed as its origin, that kamma is abandoned when greed is eliminated. It is cut off at the root, made like palm stump, obliterated so that it is no longer subject to future arising.

11 Whatever kamma, O bhikkhus, is fashioned by non-hatred, born of non-hatred, with non-hatred as its source, with non-hatred as its origin, that kamma is abandoned when hatred is eliminated. It is cut off at the root, made like palm stump, obliterated so that it is no longer subject to future arising.

12 Whatever kamma, O bhikkhus, is fashioned by non-delusion, born of non-delusion, with non-delusion as its source, with non-delusion as its origin, that kamma is abandoned when delusion is eliminated. It is cut off at the root, made like palm stump, obliterated so that it is no longer subject to future arising.

13 Just as, O bhikkhus, that there are seeds that are unbroken, unspoiled, undamaged by wind and sun, fertile, well kept. A man would burn them in a fire, having burnt it in a fire, he would make them ashes. having made them ashes, he would winnow them in a strong wind or he would cause them to be carried away in a swift flowing river. Thus, O bhikkhus, those seeds would be cut off at the root, made like palm stump, obliterated so that they are no longer subject to future arising.

This type of kamma is also described as the neither dark nor bright kamma with neither dark nor bright results which leads to the cessation of kamma (see kamma sutta AN 4.233). So once it serves its purpose of eliminating greed, hatred and delusion, which is also the cessation of kamma, then it is no longer needed and gets abandoned. And so the only remaining kamma to be depleted are the present life accumulated unwholesome kamma born of greed, hatred and delusion. As the dhammapada verses 127 states, ”a place in the world does not exists, where staying one could be released from evil kamma”, not even an arahant. The loṇaphala sutta (AN 3.100) explains how all present life accumulated unwholesome kamma come to be experienced in this very life without any residue remaining as follows:

1 Whoever, O bhikkhus, might say thus: “In which ever way that a person creates a kamma, in just that way he experiences.” That being so, O bhikkhus, there is not the living the life of purity and an opportunity for rightly making an end of suffering is not evident.

2 But whoever, O bhikkhus, might say thus: “In which ever way to be felt that a person creates a kamma, in just that way he feels the results.” That being so, O bhikkhus, there is the living the life of purity and an opportunity for rightly making an end of suffering is evident.

3 Here, O bhikkhus, for a certain person, even a trifling measure of evil kamma done leads him to hell. However here, O bhikkhus, for a certain person, that same trifling measure of evil kamma done is to be felt in this life without even a tiny [residual] to be seen, what [to speak of] very much [residual].

4 For what kind of person, O bhikkhus, does even a trifling measure of evil kamma done lead him to hell?

5 Here, O bhikkhus, a certain person is undeveloped in body, undeveloped in virtue, undeveloped in mind, and undeveloped in wisdom. He is inconsequential, depraved, and dwells in suffering over insignificant [matters]. For this kind of person, O bhikkhus, even a trifling measure of evil kamma done lead him to hell?

6 For what kind of person, O bhikkhus, does that same trifling measure of evil kamma done is to be felt in this life without even a tiny [residual] to be seen, what [to speak of] very much [residual].

7 Here, O bhikkhus, a certain person is developed in body, developed in virtue, developed in mind, and developed in wisdom. He is influential, magnanimous, and dwells in immeasurable [samadhi]. For this kind of person, that same trifling measure of evil kamma done is to be felt in this life without even a tiny [residual] to be seen, what [to speak of] very much [residual].

8 “Just as, O bhikkhus, a salt crystal were to be dropped in a small cup of water. So what do you think, O bhikkhus, would such a salt crystal make the small cup of water salty and undrinkable?”

9 Yes, Bhante.

10 What is the reason for that?

11 Because, O Bhante, however much water there is in such a small cup, such a salt crystal would make that water salty and undrinkable.”

12 “Just as, O bhikkhus, a salt crystal were to be dropped in the river Ganges. So what do you think, O bhikkhus, would such a salt crystal make the river Ganges salty and undrinkable?”

13 Certainly not, Bhante.

14 What is the reason for that?

15 “Because, O Bhante, the river Ganges is an expansive body of water, such a salt crystal would certainly not make that water salty and undrinkable.”

16 So too, O bhikkhus, here for a certain person, even a trifling measure of evil kamma done leads him to hell. However here, O bhikkhus, for a certain person, that same trifling measure of evil kamma done is to be felt in this life without even a tiny [residual] to be seen, what [to speak of] very much [residual].

The person who is developed in body, developed in virtue, developed in mind, and developed in wisdom is the arahant. Once all the present life accumulated unwholesome kamma are also fully experienced, then the arahant is entirely free from all accumulated kamma, past or present (kammanirodha), and is said to be ownerless (nirupadhī), one who eats his food free from debt (anaṇo bhuñjati bhojanaṁ) as explained in the angulimala verses:

Having done much kamma [in this life],
of the kind leading to the lower worlds;
the results of that kamma has reached me now,
and so I eat my food free from debt.
