Connected Discourses on the Aggregates: SN22:94 Flowers

  • A Saddhamma Study Group translation from the Pāḷi text.
    Get PDFs: Side-by-side | Par-by-par | Facing pages | English only | Pali only.

0 At Savatthi:

1 I, O bhikkhus, do not dispute with the world. It is indeed the world, O bhikkhus, that disputes with me. A speaker of the Dhamma, O bhikkhus, does not dispute with anyone in the world. That, O bhikkhus, which is regarded as non-existent in the world by the wise, I too say “it does not exist.” That, O bhikkhus, which is regarded as existent in the world by the wise, I too say “it does exist.”

2 And what, O bhikkhus, is regarded as non-existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does not exist?”

3 Form, O bhikkhus, that is permanent, stable, eternal, not of a nature to degenerate, is regarded as non-existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does not exist.” Feeling, O bhikkhus, that is permanent, stable, eternal, not of a nature to degenerate, is regarded as non-existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does not exist.” Perception, O bhikkhus, that is permanent, stable, eternal, not of a nature to degenerate, is regarded as non-existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does not exist.” Sankharas, O bhikkhus, that are permanent, stable, eternal, not of a nature to degenerate, is regarded as non-existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does not exist.” Consciousness, O bhikkhus, that is permanent, stable, eternal, not of a nature to degenerate, is regarded as non-existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does not exist.” This, O bhikkhus, is that which is regarded as non-existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does not exist.”

4 And what, O bhikkhus, is regarded as existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does exist?”

5 Form, O bhikkhus, that is impermanent, painful, of a nature to degenerate, is regarded as existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does exist.” Feeling, O bhikkhus, that is impermanent, painful, of a nature to degenerate, is regarded as existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does exist.” Perception, O bhikkhus, that is impermanent, painful, of a nature to degenerate, is regarded as existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does exist.” Sankharas, O bhikkhus, that are impermanent, painful, of a nature to degenerate, is regarded as existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does exist.” Consciousness, O bhikkhus, that is impermanent, painful, of a nature to degenerate, is regarded as existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does exist.” This, O bhikkhus, is that which is regarded as existent in the world by the wise, of which I too say “it does exist.”

6 There is, O bhikkhus, a worldly liability in the world which the Tathagata awakens and breaks through to. Having awoken to, having broken through to, he announces, explains, declares, establishes, reveals, analyzes, elucidates. And what, O bhikkhus, is the worldly liability in the world which the Tathagata awakens and breaks through to. Having awoken to, having broken through to, he announces, explains, declares, establishes, reveals, analyzes, elucidates?

7 Form, O bhikkhus, is a worldly liability in the world which the Tathagata awakens and breaks through to. Having awoken to, having broken through to, he announces, explains, declares, establishes, reveals, analyzes, elucidates. Whoever, O bhikkhus, does not know, does not see when the Tathagata is announcing, explaining, declaring, establishing, revealing, analyzing, elucidating thus, what, O bhikkhus, have I to do with that foolish puthujjana, blind and sightless, who [dwells] unknowing and unseeing.

8 Feeling, O bhikkhus, is a worldly liability in the world which the Tathagata awakens and breaks through to. Having awoken to, having broken through to, he announces, explains, declares, establishes, reveals, analyzes, elucidates. Whoever, O bhikkhus, does not know, does not see when the Tathagata is announcing, explaining, declaring, establishing, revealing, analyzing, elucidating thus, what, O bhikkhus, have I to do with that foolish puthujjana, blind and sightless, who [dwells] unknowing and unseeing.

9 Perception, O bhikkhus, is a worldly liability in the world which the Tathagata awakens and breaks through to. Having awoken to, having broken through to, he announces, explains, declares, establishes, reveals, analyzes, elucidates. Whoever, O bhikkhus, does not know, does not see when the Tathagata is announcing, explaining, declaring, establishing, revealing, analyzing, elucidating thus, what, O bhikkhus, have I to do with that foolish puthujjana, blind and sightless, who [dwells] unknowing and unseeing.

10 Sankharas, O bhikkhus, are a worldly liability in the world which the Tathagata awakens and breaks through to. Having awoken to, having broken through to, he announces, explains, declares, establishes, reveals, analyzes, elucidates. Whoever, O bhikkhus, does not know, does not see when the Tathagata is announcing, explaining, declaring, establishing, revealing, analyzing, elucidating thus, what, O bhikkhus, have I to do with that foolish puthujjana, blind and sightless, who [dwells] unknowing and unseeing.

11 Consciousness, O bhikkhus, is a worldly liability in the world which the Tathagata awakens and breaks through to. Having awoken to, having broken through to, he announces, explains, declares, establishes, reveals, analyzes, elucidates. Whoever, O bhikkhus, does not know, does not see when the Tathagata is announcing, explaining, declaring, establishing, revealing, analyzing, elucidating thus, what, O bhikkhus, have I to do with that foolish puthujjana, blind and sightless, who [dwells] unknowing and unseeing.

12 “Just as, O bhikkhus, a blue lotus, a red lotus, or a white lotus, born in the water, grown up in the water, rises well above the water and remains unsmeared by the water. It is just so, O bhikkhus, the Tathagata [though] born in the world, grown up in the world, dwells untarnished by the world, having overcome the world.”
